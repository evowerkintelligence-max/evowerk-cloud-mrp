# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: plant managers, ops directors, and manufacturing engineers evaluating MRP software — comparing this against Fishbowl, Katana, SAP Business One, or a spreadsheet-and-legacy-ERP patchwork, deciding whether it's worth 20 minutes to book a demo. Secondary: existing customers and investors checking credibility and depth. Demos come first; the secondary audience is served by the same evidence, not a separate track.

## Product Purpose

EvoWerk Cloud MRP is a multi-tenant, cloud-hosted manufacturing system covering BOMs/MRP explosion, procurement, lot-tracked inventory, demand forecasting, shop-floor analytics (OEE, SPC, downtime, scrap), quality/RCA, predictive maintenance, BI, data integrations, and workflow automation — built on a real security foundation (multi-tenant isolation, append-only audit log, MFA) from day one. This site exists to turn plant/ops evaluators into booked demos. It's pre-launch: no customer logos or testimonials yet, so credibility has to come from the product's actual depth and security posture, shown specifically, not claimed generically.

## Positioning

One platform for the entire plant — MRP, procurement, inventory, quality, and shop-floor analytics, without the integration duct tape. Every section should read as evidence of *one system*, never as a shelf of independent features bolted together.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Book a demo.
- Secondary fallback: see what's inside (scroll into feature depth — low-commitment, no form) for visitors not ready to book.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "One system for the whole plant — not eleven tools stitched together."
- Belief ladder: (1) the current process — spreadsheets or a legacy ERP — is actually costing them; (2) this one system covers everything they're duct-taping together; (3) it's secure and robust enough for real production use; (4) switching won't be a year-long implementation nightmare — worth 20 minutes to see it.
- Proof on hand: none yet (pre-launch). Substitute real system depth as proof: specific mechanics (audit-log immutability enforced at the DB trigger level, MRP netting logic, CI security gates) stand in for logos and testimonials.

## Brand Personality

Premium-enterprise, read through an industrial-craft lens rather than a corporate-blue one. References given: otsuka-air.jp (dark, minimal, product-photography-led, unhurried editorial pacing), coffee-tech.com (a saturated, committed brand color carrying an industrial B2B equipment site — technical credibility without looking like a data sheet), houseofhoney.com (oversized, confident display typography, warm saturated palette, editorial nerve). The common thread across all three, and the one to carry here: a single committed color doing real work, typography with genuine confidence, and editorial restraint over dashboard convention. The feeling is precision engineering, not startup cheerfulness.

## Anti-references

The generic SaaS-cream template: warm beige/near-white backgrounds, interchangeable stock-photo hero imagery, a "trusted by" logo row with no real logos. Also avoid the dated legacy-enterprise look (dense gray toolbars, corporate blue-and-white, 2012-era chrome) — premium-enterprise means neither of those.

## Design Principles

Prove depth through specificity, not adjectives — with no logos or testimonials yet, credibility comes from naming real mechanics (the DB-level audit trigger, MRP explosion, MFA) instead of vague trust claims.

Pain before pitch — open by naming the real cost of spreadsheets or a legacy ERP before introducing the product, matching the belief ladder's order.

One system, shown as one system — every section reinforces breadth without fragmentation; nothing should read as an independent feature module.

Industrial confidence, not startup cheerfulness — match the audience's professional register: plant and ops leaders, not consumer SaaS shoppers.

Show, don't dashboard — favor real interface/data-shaped visuals over icon-grid feature lists.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast throughout. Every animation ships a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative (crossfade or instant), not just a suppressed reflex.
